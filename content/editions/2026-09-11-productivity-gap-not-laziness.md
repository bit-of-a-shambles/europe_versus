Europeans work fewer hours than Americans. That fact is often stretched into a claim about how hard or how well they work. The OECD's hourly data makes the distinction clear. In 2024, the EU produced **$63.4 of output per hour**, against $84.1 in the United States. The aggregate gap was real and large: about 25% of the US level.

Yet the country ranking does not resemble a continent-wide work ethic. Denmark produced $92.2 per hour and Belgium $90.9. Switzerland, Norway and the Netherlands also sat near or above the American level. Austria, Germany, Sweden and France were close behind. At the other end, Greece produced $38.4 per hour and Latvia $45.1.

Productivity is output divided by time worked. It reflects capital, technology, management, infrastructure and the industries in which people work. It is not a measure of effort or virtue. A nurse with poor equipment does not become more productive by skipping lunch; a factory with better machinery can produce more in the same hour without anyone moving faster.

The top of the ranking needs two warnings. Ireland and Luxembourg report extraordinary GDP per hour because multinational profits and cross-border work inflate domestic output. The OECD's alternative using gross national income lowers Ireland from $135.7 to $102.0 and Luxembourg from $108.0 to $70.6. Norway also benefits from a highly productive petroleum sector. Denmark and Belgium are cleaner evidence that European economies can operate at the frontier.

The chart does not rescue the EU average. A large share of Europe's workers live in countries well below the frontier, while the United States combines a high hourly level with a much larger unified market. Nor should this chart be used to compare annual hours between countries; the OECD warns that those estimates are not harmonised well enough for level comparisons.

So cancel the morality play. Europe's problem is the slow spread of productive firms and practices across sectors and borders. The frontier already exists inside Europe. Too few workers and companies get to operate on it. Calling that laziness explains nothing and suggests the wrong cure.
